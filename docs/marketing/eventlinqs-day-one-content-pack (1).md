## RESEARCH DECISIONS (what was adopted, what was rejected, and why)

**ADOPTED from the external research:**
1. **Publish the full 9-post grid before outreach begins.** Correct and now scheduled below. An empty grid kills DM conversion because every prospect checks the profile within 60 seconds.
2. **The co-listing de-risk offer.** New objection response, added to the playbook: prospects terrified of leaving Eventbrite can list on both platforms and allocate a portion of tickets to EventLinqs as a test run. This is a genuinely strong friction-killer for the most nervous segment.
3. **The soft-open first message.** A no-pitch first DM variant that leads with their event, not our platform. Added to the playbook as an A/B variant against the direct offer DM.

**REJECTED, with reasons:**
1. **"LinkedIn and email only for organisers, Instagram only for artists."** Wrong for our founding segment. Comedy rooms, market organisers, run clubs, gig promoters and community groups in Geelong and Melbourne live on Instagram and Facebook, and many have no LinkedIn presence at all. Rule instead: match the channel to where the prospect is actually active. LinkedIn is the founder's build-in-public channel and the venue-manager channel, not the primary DM channel.
2. **The "5 local pioneers, zero fees for their first event" script.** This would create a second, competing offer that deviates from the locked Founding Organiser terms (zero platform fees for 6 months, extendable by 3 months per successful organiser referral, capped at the first 50 organisers in Geelong and Melbourne). One offer, one wording, everywhere. A prospect who sees two different deals stops trusting both.
3. **GST advice does not touch the copy.** The platform's fee display is already GST-inclusive under its verified limited-collection-agent posture, computed from the live fee code. No marketing copy adds 10%, implies a separate GST line, or changes any verified figure. Whether the business registers for GST and claims input credits is an accountant question for Lawal, not a marketing decision, and it changes nothing about what the buyer sees.

Daily stories, pick 2 to 3 per day: a poll ("What is the worst thing about ticketing fees?"), a this-or-that on local events, a behind-the-scenes build shot, a repost of a local Geelong or Melbourne event with genuine praise and a tag.

Daily engagement warm-up, 30 minutes: follow 20 to 30 local organisers, venues and event pages across both cities, and leave genuine comments on 10 of them. No pitching in comments, ever. This is the goodwill layer that makes the eventual DM a warm knock instead of a cold one.

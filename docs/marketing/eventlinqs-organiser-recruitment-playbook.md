# EventLinqs Organiser Recruitment Playbook
## Mission: 50 organiser conversions per week, recruiting where the founder has reach (Geelong and Melbourne) on a platform that is national from day one, bootstrapped, zero ad spend
### Version 1.0, 4 July 2026. Offer rewritten to LAW 24 as ruled, 26 September 2026. Australian English. No em-dashes or en-dashes. "Community" always, never the banned word.

---

## PART 0: THE HONEST NUMBERS BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE

50 signed organisers per week from cold outreach is a funnel, not a wish. Industry-realistic cold outreach conversion for an unknown platform is 2 to 5 percent from first touch to signed up and active. That means:

- 50 organisers per week requires roughly 1,000 to 2,500 quality touches per week.
- A solo founder doing manual, personalised DMs can realistically do 30 to 60 quality touches per day, so 200 to 400 per week.
- Manual alone gets you 8 to 20 organisers per week at first. That is the honest starting point.

The path to 50 per week is therefore: manual outreach to build the first 20 to 40 founding organisers, then let the referral and density loops multiply it. The Founding Organiser Program below is designed to make each signed organiser recruit the next two. Weeks 1 to 4 target 10 to 15 per week. Weeks 5 to 8 target 25 to 35. Weeks 9 onward target 50, with roughly half coming from referral and public proof rather than cold touches.

Hard rule: no outreach at scale until Tab 2 (paid path) and Tab 3 (verification) are green. Sending 500 DMs to a platform where checkout fails burns the Geelong market permanently. It is a small, connected community of organisers. You get one first impression. Warm-up outreach (list building, following, engaging with organiser content) can start today. Pitching starts the day the paid path is proven.

## PART 1: POSITIONING. WHY ORGANISERS SWITCH.

Organisers do not switch platforms for vibes. They switch for one of five reasons: money, control, audience, support, or pain with their current platform. EventLinqs must lead with a concrete offer on at least two of these.

### The core pitch (one sentence)
EventLinqs is the Australian platform where local events get made: lower cost, faster setup with AI assistance, and a growing local audience in your own city.

### The five attack angles, mapped to competitor weaknesses

1. **Money (vs Eventbrite, Ticketek).** Eventbrite's fees and payout timing are the single most complained-about thing in Australian organiser communities. If EventLinqs can publicly commit to a lower all-in fee, lead every message with the dollar difference on a real example: "On a $30 ticket for 200 people, here is what you keep with us vs them." FOUNDER DECISION REQUIRED: lock the public fee structure before outreach begins. Without a published fee, the strongest weapon is holstered.

2. **The Founding Organiser offer (solves the cold start).** Every organiser in Australia gets zero platform fees for 6 months, counted from the date they register, with no cap, no limit on places and no invitation needed, plus 3 more fee-free months for each organiser they refer who sells a ticket, on top of the 6 months. When the free months end, the standard fee applies. Every organiser receives the Founding Organiser name and badge, and input into the roadmap. There is no scarcity to sell and none is ever implied: the value is real, and it converts "why would I join an empty platform" into "I pay nothing while I find out."

3. **Speed and help (the AI layer as a selling point).** "Create a professional event page in under five minutes, with an assistant that helps write your description and pick your categories." No competitor in the local segment sells this well yet. It is a genuine differentiator once Tab 5 ships.

4. **Local density (the Launch Density Layer as a promise to organisers).** "EventLinqs is national and built local: discovery is organised by city and suburb, so the people browsing your city are your actual local audience, not a national firehose where your event is buried on page nine."

5. **Human support from the founder.** Against Eventbrite's support reputation, "message me directly and I will fix it today" is a weapon only a bootstrapped platform has. Use it loudly while it is still true.

### Segments to target, in priority order
1. Organisers currently on Eventbrite running recurring events under 500 tickets (highest fee pain, easiest migration).
2. Community groups, run clubs, markets, food and music nights in Geelong (density beachhead, tight networks, fast word of mouth).
3. Small venues and promoters in Melbourne's inner suburbs (comedy rooms, live music nights, trivia).
4. University clubs and societies (semester 2 events being planned right now in July).
5. Fitness, wellness and workshop organisers (recurring events, sticky once onboarded).

Artists come after organisers. Organisers bring events; events bring artists and buyers. Do not split focus in the first eight weeks.

## PART 2: CREDIBILITY FLOOR. BUILD BEFORE PITCHING.

A prospect who receives a DM will check three things within 60 seconds. All three must pass:

1. **The website.** Home page must load fast, look professional (Tab 4's job), show real-looking events, and have a clear "For organisers" page stating fees, payout timing, and the Founding Organiser Program. If the fee page does not exist, build it before outreach.
2. **The founder's LinkedIn.** Lawal's profile is the trust anchor for a bootstrapped platform. It should say founder of EventLinqs, show 2 or 3 posts about building the platform, and look active. People buy from people when the brand is unknown.
3. **One social profile with life in it.** Pick Instagram as primary (event organisers live there). 9 to 12 posts before outreach starts: platform screenshots, the founding organiser offer, one founder-to-camera or founder-story post. Add local event community content from Geelong and Melbourne. It does not need followers. It needs to not look abandoned.

Also required before scale: **one real, public, demonstrable event on the platform.** Run your own free launch event ("EventLinqs Founding Organisers Meetup, Geelong") if no organiser is live yet. It is a demo, a proof point, and a recruitment event in one.

## PART 3: THE OUTREACH ENGINE

### Sourcing (where the 1,000 touches come from)
- Eventbrite and Humanitix public listings filtered to Geelong and Melbourne: every recurring organiser is a lead with a visible event history.
- Instagram: location tags (Geelong, Geelong West, Torquay, Fitzroy, Brunswick, Footscray), hashtags like #geelongevents #melbourneevents #melbournemarkets #melbournegigs.
- Facebook Events and local community groups (Geelong Community Noticeboard type groups are organiser goldmines).
- Council event calendars (City of Greater Geelong, City of Melbourne) list organisers with contact details.
- Meetup.com groups in both cities.
- Gumtree and local newspaper what's-on listings for the long tail.

Build a simple CRM spreadsheet with: organiser name, event type, city, current platform, contact channel, personalisation note, touch dates, status. Every touch is logged. What is not measured does not improve.

### Cadence per lead
- Day 0: engage first (follow, genuinely comment on their event post). Warm, not creepy, one comment.
- Day 1: personalised DM (templates below).
- Day 4: light follow-up.
- Day 8: email if findable, different angle to the DM.
- Day 15: final value touch (share something useful, no ask), then park for 60 days.
Three asks maximum. Never argue, never guilt.

### Daily operating rhythm (solo founder)
- 60 minutes: source and log 40 new leads.
- 90 minutes: send 30 to 40 personalised first touches.
- 30 minutes: follow-ups.
- 30 minutes: replies and demo calls booked.
- 15 minutes: post one piece of content (platform progress, organiser tip, local event spotlight).
- Friday: review the numbers, kill the worst-performing template, double the best.

### Metrics that matter (review every Friday)
Touches sent, reply rate (target 15 percent or better on DMs; below 8 percent means the message or the profile is the problem), demo or call rate, signup rate, first-event-live rate, referral count. The playbook is rewritten every Friday based on these. That is the "reevaluate and improve" loop, made mechanical.

## PART 4: TEMPLATES

Rules for all copy: Australian English, no em-dashes or en-dashes, "community" never the banned word, no hype words like revolutionary or disrupt, every message personalised in the first line, one clear ask, short.

### Instagram or Facebook DM, first touch (Eventbrite organiser)
> Hey [name], came across [event name] and it looks like a great night. Quick one from one local to another: I have built EventLinqs, a new Australian platform where events get made. Every organiser gets zero platform fees for their first 6 months from the day they register, no cap and no invitation needed. Keen to show you what you would save on your ticket prices? Takes 10 minutes.

### DM, first touch (community group or free events)
> Hey [name], love what you are doing with [group/event]. I am a Melbourne founder building EventLinqs, an Australian platform for local community events, open in every city. Free events are always free on the platform, setup takes minutes, and paid events carry no platform fee for your first 6 months. Would you be open to me setting up [event name] for you as a demo, no strings?

### DM, follow-up (day 4)
> Hey [name], just floating this back up. Happy to send a 2 minute video of how [event name] would look on EventLinqs if that is easier than a call. Either way, good luck with the event.

### Cold email, first touch
> Subject: [event name] with lower ticket fees
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I found [event name] on [platform] and wanted to reach out directly. I am Lawal, founder of EventLinqs, a new Australian event platform, open in every city today.
>
> For a $[price] ticket, your attendees currently pay about $[their fee] in fees. On EventLinqs every organiser pays zero platform fees for 6 months from the day they register, plus 3 more fee-free months for each organiser they refer who sells a ticket.
>
> Worth a 10 minute look this week? Reply with a time or just "send details" and I will send a short video instead.
>
> Cheers,
> Lawal
> Founder, EventLinqs

### Objection responses (memorise these)
- "Nobody is on your platform." That is exactly why every organiser pays no platform fee for their first 6 months. And discovery is organised by city, so your event is front and centre for your local audience, not buried. Plus you keep selling through your existing channels; the ticket link just costs you less.
- "Switching is a hassle." Building your event takes minutes, and you can run it alongside your current listing while you try us. [FOUNDER DECISION, 26 September 2026: hand migration was promised to a capped group; decide whether it is offered to every organiser before promising it again.]
- "How do I know my money is safe?" Payments run on Stripe, the same payment infrastructure the big platforms use, and payouts go straight to your account. Happy to walk you through it on a call.
- "I will think about it." No worries. There is no deadline and no cap: your 6 months start whenever you register, so they are yours whenever you are ready.

### Referral ask (send after their first successful event)
> [name], congrats on [event]. [X] tickets through the platform, and everything ran clean. Quick ask: who is one other organiser in [city] who would want the same deal? When they sell their first ticket, your zero-fee period extends by 3 months, on top of your 6, and that applies for each organiser you refer.

This referral mechanic is the engine that gets from 15 per week to 50 per week. Ask every single time, without exception.

## PART 5: PUBLIC PROOF CONTENT (the air cover for the DMs)

Three posts per week minimum, rotating:
1. **Build-in-public founder posts (LinkedIn):** shipping updates, honest lessons, and the number of organisers who have joined. This is free trust at scale. Never a countdown and never manufactured urgency: the offer has no cap and no deadline.
2. **Organiser spotlights (Instagram):** every founding organiser gets a professional-looking spotlight post. It rewards them, markets their event, and shows the next prospect that real organisers are here.
3. **Local event community content:** weekend event roundups for Geelong and Melbourne, even including events not on the platform yet. This builds the buyer-side audience and gives a genuine reason for organisers to want in.

## PART 6: FLAWS AND GAPS THE PRODUCTION TEAM MUST HEAR (marketing's honest audit)

These are the things that will quietly cap growth if not addressed. Ranked by impact.

1. **Standard fee structure still undecided.** The Founding Organiser offer is LAW 24 as ruled, 26 September 2026: Every organiser in Australia gets zero platform fees for 6 months, counted from the date they register, with no cap, no limit on places and no invitation needed, plus 3 more fee-free months for each organiser they refer who sells a ticket, on top of the 6 months. When the free months end, the standard fee applies. Every organiser receives the Founding Organiser name and badge. No copy may deviate from this wording. But the standard fee structure that applies after the 6 months remains undecided. The fee comparison table, the FAQ, and the "what happens after 6 months" objection all fire blanks until it is locked. Still the highest-priority founder decision.
2. **No migration tooling.** Migrating organisers by hand does not scale to an offer with no cap. Tabs should eventually build an attendee CSV import and an event import flow. Flag it for the roadmap now; it is the biggest switching-friction killer in this market.
3. **Organiser landing page: CORRECTED, not a gap.** The page exists and is live, and is being redesigned to world-class standard in the current UI sprint. Marketing's job is conversion copy for the redesigned page (see the companion landing page copy document). The remaining ask to production: ensure the redesigned page has slots for fees, payout timing, the Founding Organiser offer, and testimonials, because the copy depends on those sections existing.
4. **No social proof mechanism in-product.** Ticket buyer counts, organiser testimonials, and a founding organiser badge need product support. Small features, huge conversion impact.
5. **Payout timing is undefined in every strategy document.** Organisers ask "when do I get my money" in the first five minutes of every conversation. The Stripe Connect setup determines the answer; it must be known, fast, and published.
6. **No referral tracking.** The referral loop is the growth engine. Even a spreadsheet works at first, but the platform should eventually track referred organisers and auto-extend fee-free periods.
7. **Email capture on the buyer side.** Every ticket buyer should be a marketable local audience member (with consent). Confirm the platform captures marketing consent at checkout, because the "local audience density" pitch to organisers depends on actually having one.
8. **The 7 July webhook deadline and outreach timing collide.** Do not let launch excitement start the pitching before Tabs 2 and 3 are green. Marketing's first deliverable this week is lists and profiles, not sent messages.

## PART 7: FIRST 14 DAYS, DAY BY DAY

- **Days 1 to 2:** Founder locks the standard fee structure (the offer is LAW 24 as ruled, 26 September 2026: zero platform fees for 6 months from registration for every organiser, plus 3 more fee-free months for each organiser referred who sells a ticket, no cap). Build the CRM sheet. Source first 200 leads. Set up or refresh Instagram and LinkedIn.
- **Days 3 to 4:** Write and publish the For Organisers page (coordinate with Tab 4). Post first 9 Instagram posts. First LinkedIn build-in-public post.
- **Day 5:** Platform verification green (Tabs 2 and 3). Run one internal end-to-end demo event. Record a 2 minute demo video for DM follow-ups.
- **Days 6 to 10:** Outreach live. 30 to 40 first touches per day, Geelong first. Book demos. Target: first 5 organisers signed.
- **Days 11 to 14:** First organiser events being set up. First spotlight posts. First referral asks. Friday review: reply rates by template and segment, rewrite the weakest, scale the strongest.

Target at day 14: 10 to 15 organisers signed, 3 to 5 events live, build-in-public content running, and a repeatable machine whose numbers tell us exactly what to fix next week. That machine, improved every Friday, is what gets to 50 per week and then carries the platform national.

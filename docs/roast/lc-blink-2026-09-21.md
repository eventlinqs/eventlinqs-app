## The five defects found and fixed in this pass, for row 9

1. THE ITEM ITSELF. Fifteen reads in src/lib/notifications discarding their
   error, across five files.

2. THE DIGEST CLAIM ORDER, found by the adversarial pass rather than by the
   scan, and the most expensive of the set. The daily digest claims the day by
   appending to `organiser_sales_digest_sends` BEFORE it sends, deliberately, so
   that a crash costs one digest rather than a duplicate money summary. Two
   READS sat after that claim, and both of them raise. So one dropped socket
   spent the day, landed in the per-organisation catch, and the organiser never
   received that digest and never would: the next run finds the claim row and
   counts it as already sent. Every read is before the claim now.

3. MY OWN GUARD, twice. It stripped string bodies and then searched for a string
   literal, so its first run reported three faults against the three files that
   carried the fix correctly. And clause 1 used a substring test that a rename
   walked straight past, which drill 1 caught.

4. MY OWN DRIVE called `auth.admin.deleteUser` directly, which
   `one-way-to-delete-an-account` refused by name. That call failed silently for
   every account on the platform for a day in September 2026 while every drive
   still printed a clean teardown.

5. MY OWN DRILLS. One anchor was stale, and one drill tested a clause that could
   not be made to fail. The clause was deleted rather than reworded until the
   drill stopped complaining.
